Things I Still Remember

Not everything I remember from PBS Kids is tied to one specific game. Some of it is just a sound, a menu, a character, a website layout, or something tiny that stayed with me for years.

I Was Here for the Games

Interestingly, I did not really watch PBS Kids growing up despite spending so much time on its website. I was mainly there for the games. The only PBS Kids show I remember watching for a while was Maya & Miguel, but even that was not something I watched for very long.

When it came to television, I was much more of a Cartoon Network, Nickelodeon, and Boomerang kid. Because of that, most of my familiarity with shows like Arthur, Wild Kratts, Martha Speaks, Dinosaur Train, and Curious George actually came through their games rather than the shows themselves.

Website Design

One of the things I remember most about the old PBS Kids website was the large character wheel in the middle of the homepage. I liked hovering over the arrows and cycling through the different shows until I found the franchise I wanted.

I usually spent most of my time in the Games section, either browsing from the main homepage or going directly into the pages for specific shows. I also liked that the individual franchise pages did not all look the same. Their layouts, colors, and decorations were usually designed around the shows themselves, which made each section feel like its own little world instead of just another page on the same website.

Sounds & Music

Surprisingly, a lot of the PBS Kids games I remember did not use much background music. Wild Kratts stood out the most because its games often had more intense music that matched how much attention they required, especially during competitive or fast-paced sections.

The smaller sound effects are what bring back stronger memories for me. In Martha Speaks: Crazy Vehicle, the sound of clicking the different transformation buttons immediately felt familiar when I played it again. It is funny how something as simple as a button sound can bring back a whole chunk of childhood.

Things I Forgot About

One franchise I completely forgot to mention at first was Sid the Science Kid, even though I played a lot of its games growing up. I remember them being especially relaxing to play while still teaching you something, which made them stand out from some of the more fast-paced games on the site.

Most of the games I remember seem to be lost to time now, although a few are still available. Revisiting them reminded me how memorable the franchise actually was for me, especially because the games managed to feel educational without making the learning part feel overwhelming.
