Games I Can Revisit

These are games I can still find and replay today. I’m writing about what I remembered, what I forgot and how they feel now compared to when I was younger.

Arthur: Buster Baxter Lung Defender

Available

I remember struggling with the timing when trying to catch the viruses, though playing it again now, it was much simpler than I remembered.

Read more

For some reason, I remembered this game as being part of a completely different franchise, so I was surprised to find out that it was actually an Arthur game. I did not play many Arthur games growing up, with the only other one I remember being Go! George Go! That game confused me a lot as a kid, so I never played it very much. Lung Defender was much easier for me to understand, and I remember coming back to it pretty often.

The controls are simple and the goal of the game is easy to understand, while still being fairly educational. Playing it again, I did notice that catching the viruses can feel a little unresponsive unless you press the mouse button all the way down. I also remembered how cute the viruses looked, which was probably part of why I enjoyed the game so much as a kid.

Wild Kratts: Kickboxing Kangaroo!

Available

I remember playing this game with my sibling, and we would laugh uncontrollably at how ridiculous the fighting mechanics were.

Read more

I played a lot of Wild Kratts games growing up, and this was one of my favorites. The premise was pretty simple: you had to fight a kangaroo as a kangaroo. Even so, it was still incredibly engaging and fun to me as a kid.

Playing it now is still just as fun, and the fighting mechanics are every bit as ridiculous as I remembered. There is also a surprising amount of difficulty to it. I remember struggling with having to keep track of both your stamina and your opponent's while also trying to land hits at the right time.

The kangaroo's blocking mechanic was probably the funniest part of the game to me, but it could also be frustrating since you couldn't land a hit while they were blocking. Looking back, that mix of silliness and actual challenge was probably a big part of why I kept coming back to it.

Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood: Tea Party

Available

I remember enjoying this game a lot because of the tea party theme and how much freedom it gave you to choose the table setup. I suppose this is one of the places where my creative side started to show, since I would spend a ridiculous amount of time picking the tablecloth, cups, teapot, and snacks.

Read more

There isn't a huge amount to the game, but I still find it enjoyable now. The atmosphere is very relaxed, and Daniel's commentary gives the whole thing a lot of charm. I also really liked that you could actually eat the snacks and drink the tea yourself instead of only watching the characters do it. Everything looked incredibly delicious to me as a kid.

Looking back, I do find it pretty funny that all the kids mostly just sit there and stare at you, though that never bothered me when I was younger. One memory that still makes me laugh is my sibling getting annoyed whenever Miss Elaina said "toots" over and over, which naturally made me find it even funnier.

Dinosaur Train: All Aboard!

Available

Looking back, Dinosaur Train definitely helped shape my love for dinosaurs at a young age, and that interest has carried over to this day. I was also surprised by how educational the franchise really was. All Aboard! is quite a short game, even though I remember it feeling much longer as a kid, which was probably because I replayed it so many times.

Read more

Even though the game is short, it is still fun to play as an adult. I like how it teaches you which dinosaurs are carnivores or herbivores without making the educational part feel too heavy. I also really like how creative the mouse icons are when you board the dinosaurs, collect their tickets, feed them, and drop them off at their stops. Each one fits the dinosaur theme really well and makes the game feel more cohesive.

I especially enjoyed seeing the wide variety of dinosaurs in this game and across the franchise in general. Their colorful coats, patterns, and different skin textures were always very eye-catching to me, and I remember being fascinated by how distinct each dinosaur looked when I was a kid.

Curious George: ZOOmazing

Available

ZOOmazing is one of the Curious George games I remember playing the most as a kid. Even though the premise is pretty simple, I remember finding it slightly difficult at the time. Looking back, it may have also helped kickstart my love of zoos and wild animals, which has stuck with me ever since.

Read more

The goal of the game is to guide different animals back to their homes by moving them across the board one space at a time. It is very straightforward now, but I remember having to think much harder about the routes when I was younger.

Playing it again, I also noticed something I never really thought about as a kid: the game can feel slightly creepy when you are moving the animals around. There is no background music during those sections, so all you hear are the sound effects every time the animal moves from one space to another, almost like you are moving a board game piece through an otherwise completely quiet zoo.

Despite that slightly eerie feeling, I still find the game charming, especially because of how much it focuses on different animals and their habitats. It makes sense to me now why I was so drawn to it.

Martha Speaks: Crazy Vehicle

Available

I do not remember as much about Crazy Vehicle as some of the other games I played growing up, but I do remember finding it entertaining to transform the vehicle for each situation. There was also something oddly satisfying about clicking the different buttons and watching the vehicle change.

Read more

One thing I appreciate about the game is that Martha tells you which button to press for every scenario. Some of the options are not immediately obvious, especially for a younger kid, so the guidance makes the game much easier to understand.

Playing it now, though, that same guidance starts to become a little repetitive once you get used to the rhythm of the game. It makes sense for the intended audience, but as an adult I found myself knowing what to do before Martha finished explaining it.

Even with that, I still find the transforming mechanic charming. The game is simple, but the combination of the sound effects, buttons, and changing vehicle gives it a satisfying little loop that I can see why I enjoyed as a kid.

Sesame Street: Zoe's Dance Moves

Available

Zoe's Dance Moves was not one of the PBS Kids games I played as often, mostly because it was fairly short and I did not spend as much time with Sesame Street games in general. I do remember really liking the colorful, girly interface though, especially the part where you could pick an outfit for Zoe before starting.

Read more

The game itself is pretty simple, but I can see why I enjoyed it as a kid. Dressing Zoe up before the dance made the whole thing feel a little more personal, and the bright interface matched the playful tone really well.

Playing it again as an adult, I also appreciate how much variety there actually is in the dance styles. It does not stop at things like hip-hop and ballet, but also includes African and Asian dances, tap dancing, and even a deliberately silly dance. That gives the game more personality than I remembered and makes it feel a little broader than a basic dress-up-and-dance game.

The Ruff Ruffman Show: Germinator

Available

Germinator is the only game from The Ruff Ruffman Show that I clearly remember playing, and for years I actually mixed it up with Arthur: Buster Baxter Lung Defender because of their similar premises. I remember finding Germinator pretty difficult as a kid, but I still enjoyed playing it and managed to make my way through it.

Read more

What I especially like about Germinator is that it does more with the idea of invading someone's immune system than I remembered. Instead of entering through only one route, you approach the body in several different ways depending on the level, which gives the game more variety and makes the premise feel more playful.

Playing it again, I found the difficulty to be pretty well balanced. It is challenging enough that you have to pay attention, but not so difficult that completing it feels frustrating. That surprised me a little considering how much harder I remembered it being when I was younger.

Looking back, I can definitely see why my brain blended this game together with Lung Defender. Both revolve around germs and the body, but replaying them now made me realize that they actually have pretty distinct approaches to the same basic idea.

Clifford the Big Red Dog: Clifford's Big Parade

Available

Clifford's Big Parade is another creativity-focused game I loved as a kid. The premise is very straightforward: you choose a theme for your parade float, pick decorations that match it, and place them wherever you want before watching the finished float appear in the parade.

Read more

There is not much challenge to the game, but that was never really the point for me. I liked being able to choose a theme and then decide how I wanted the float to look. Placing all the different decorations made it feel a little like building a tiny scene.

The most satisfying part was seeing the finished float in action at the end. After spending time arranging everything, getting that little payoff made the whole process feel complete. Looking back, I can see why I kept coming back to games like this that gave me room to customize and make something of my own.

WordGirl: Pretty Princess Magical Rescue Adventure Game

Available

Pretty Princess Magical Rescue Adventure Game was one of the WordGirl games I played a lot growing up. I really liked how unapologetically girly the game looked, while still giving you the option to play as Becky's younger brother when rescuing the princess.

Read more

One of my favorite things about the game was how much choice it gave you along the way. You could decide how you wanted to rescue the princess and which paths to take, which made the adventure feel a little different depending on what you picked.

I also liked how those choices eventually led into the final "boss fight," which is much less dramatic than that name makes it sound. Instead of actually fighting anything, you have to describe the locations correctly by choosing the right word. It is a clever way of tying the vocabulary-learning aspect of WordGirl into the ending without making it feel completely separate from the adventure.

I remembered the game being much longer when I was younger, but replaying it now, it is actually pretty short. That seems to be a recurring theme with a lot of these games, though I probably stretched them out as a kid by replaying different choices and taking my time with everything.

Lost to Time

I remember playing games from these shows, but I haven’t been able to find the specific ones I remember anymore.

Super Why!

Super Why is one of the few franchises where I have not been able to properly revisit the games I remember playing, either because they are no longer available or have been reworked into newer versions. Princess Presto was always my favorite character, and one of the games I remember most clearly is Princess Presto's Golden Crown Spelling Bee.

Read more

What I remember liking most was being able to create colorful jewels for Princess Presto's crown while working through the spelling activities. The game was educational, but the creative part was what really kept my attention because it felt like I was making something instead of simply answering questions.

I also remember loving that you could print out the finished crown afterward. That made the game feel more tangible than a lot of other browser games, since something you created on the computer could actually leave the screen and become something you could keep.

Unfortunately, I have not been able to find the original version I remember well enough to replay it properly, so most of this entry is based on what has stuck with me from childhood. Even then, the fact that I still remember decorating those jewels and printing the crown says a lot about what I enjoyed most about it.
